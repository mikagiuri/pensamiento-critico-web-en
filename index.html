Critical Thinking

Stories to think with

Fables and short tales from every tradition—Socrates, Zhuangzi, Aesop, Tolstoy, an Inuit tale, a Chinese parable—to read in five minutes and think about for a whole lesson: each comes with comprehension questions, reflection questions and a journal entry.

Visual

Infographics

Visual boards by topic, in HTML: to project in class or to review at a glance. Editable and always growing.

Tool

Concept maps

Interactive mind maps: click a node (or the circle) to collapse or expand branches, drag to move around and use the wheel to zoom. Ideal for projecting in class or reviewing at a glance.

Visual

Gallery

The drawings from the Stories to Think With: one public-domain image per story. Click one to view it full screen; use ‹ › to move through them.

Reference

Glossary

The subject’s dictionary: 301 terms from History of Philosophy with their definitions, taken from the department’s textbook. Search by word or filter by block and area.

Tool

Cards

Revision with cards: click the card (or press the space bar) to turn it over. Mark the ones you have mastered; your progress is saved in this browser.

Tool

Quizzes

Self-assessment with instant marking and an explanation of each answer. Your best score is saved in this browser.

Tool

Juego · Reto

Revise by playing: against the clock, with points for speed and streaks. Your best score is saved in this browser.

Tool

Pairs

Match each glossary term with its definition, against the clock. Earn points and streaks; your best score is saved in this browser.

Ethics

Ethical dilemmas

Situations in which you have to choose between two things that matter. Choose, give your reason and see whether you change your mind. ‘Class mode’ counts the classroom’s votes.

Game

If the class were the world

Your class as a miniature planet: guess how many of you would go hungry, have no drinking water or not go to school, and then check.

Game

Choose your path

Stories in which you decide what happens. Each decision leads somewhere else and there are several endings: discover them all and think about why each one ends the way it does.

Exercise

Progressive hints

Key questions from the syllabus to answer on your own, with hints that are revealed one at a time: from the vaguest to the one that almost gives you the answer. If they run out, a text and an explanation help you get there.

Exercise

Logic Corner: dirty tricks

The tricks for winning an argument without being right, with caricatures and museum paintings. Learn to recognise them so they are not played on you.
